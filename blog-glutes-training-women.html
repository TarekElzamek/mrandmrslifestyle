Glute Training for Women: What Actually Works

By Chloe Antoniazzi, women's & pre/postnatal coach  ·  8 min read  ·  Updated October 2026
← Back to Blog

The short answer: train your glutes 2–3 times a week, combine a hip-thrust or hinge movement with a squat or lunge movement, take most sets close to failure, and add a little weight or a rep every week. Eat around 1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of body weight and sleep 7+ hours. Done consistently, most women see a visible change in 8–12 weeks.

Glute training is one of the most searched topics in women's fitness, and one of the most confusing. Social media is full of 50-rep band circuits and "secret" exercises. In my coaching, the women who get results are rarely doing anything fancy. They do a few proven exercises, track their weights, and keep showing up.

This guide is the approach I use with my clients in Dubai and online: what to do, how much, and what to ignore.

Why most glute workouts don't work

Too light, for too long

Your glutes are the biggest muscle group in your body. A light band does very little once you can easily do 30 reps. Muscle grows when the work is genuinely hard. You can build muscle with heavier weights for 6–10 reps or lighter weights for 15–30 reps, but either way the last 2–3 reps of the set should be a real struggle.

No progression

Doing the same workout with the same weights for months tells your body there's no reason to change. Write your weights down and aim to beat last week by one rep or a small jump in weight.

Only one type of exercise

You'll often hear that squats "don't work the glutes". That isn't true. Deep squats and lunges train the glutes hard, especially at the bottom of the movement, while hip thrusts work them hardest at the top. The best results come from doing both kinds of movement, not picking one.

Cardio instead of strength work

Walking, running and spin are great for your heart and for fat loss, but they don't give your glutes enough resistance to grow. Keep your cardio, and add strength training on top of it.

The 3 things that actually build glutes

1. Hard sets and progressive overload

Every working set should end 1–3 reps short of failure. Each week, add a rep, add weight, or slow the lowering phase. Small increases add up to real change over a few months.

2. Enough weekly volume

For most women, 10–20 hard sets for the glutes per week, split across 2–3 sessions, is the sweet spot. Beginners can start at the lower end, around 8–10 sets.

3. Protein, food and sleep

Muscle is built while you recover, not during the workout. Aim for 1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of body weight each day. If you're also dieting hard, glute growth will be slower, so be patient. Our free calorie calculator will give you your daily targets.

A simple weekly glute plan

This works alongside your normal upper-body training. Rest about 2 minutes between heavy sets and 60–90 seconds on the lighter ones.

Day 1 — Heavy hips

Hip thrust 4 × 8–10 · Romanian deadlift 3 × 8–10 · Cable kickback 3 × 12–15 · Hip abduction 2 × 15–20

Day 2 — Legs & single-leg

Squat or leg press 3 × 8–12 · Bulgarian split squat 3 × 10 each leg · 45° back extension 3 × 12–15

Optional Day 3 — Pump & control

Hip thrust (lighter, pause at the top) 3 × 12–15 · Step-ups 3 × 10 each leg · Hip abduction 3 × 20. You can also swap this for a reformer Pilates session for core and hip control.

Training at home? Swap the barbell hip thrust for a dumbbell or single-leg version on your sofa, the leg press for goblet squats, and the cables for a heavy loop band.

Things that are specific to women

Your menstrual cycle

You don't need a special "cycle-synced" programme to make progress. Keep training all month. If energy dips in the days before or during your period, it's fine to drop a set or go a little lighter, then push again when you feel strong.

After pregnancy

Glute training is one of the best things you can do after having a baby. It helps with back pain from lifting and feeding, and with pelvic stability. Get clearance from your doctor first, usually from around six weeks after birth. Look out for leaking, heaviness in the pelvis or "doming" of the stomach. If you notice any of these, reduce the load and get the pelvic floor checked by a women's health physio. As a certified pre and postnatal coach, I start new mums with lighter hip thrusts and bridges, focusing on breathing, before building up the weight.

Knees and hips

Weak side glutes are a common reason for knees caving in on squats and for hip or knee niggles. Abduction work and single-leg exercises fix this, so don't skip them.

Training your glutes in Dubai

  • Beat the crowds. Hip thrust benches and Smith machines are busiest from 6–9pm. Early mornings or late evenings make it much easier to stick to your plan.
  • Summer heat. From June to September, keep your training indoors, drink more water than you think you need, and add electrolytes if you're sweating heavily, so your strength doesn't drop.
  • Ladies-only options. If you'd rather train away from a busy mixed gym, I coach 1-to-1 as a female personal trainer at The Pilates Room by Chloe in Meydan, a ladies-only studio where babies are welcome too.

How long until you see results?

With consistent training, rising weights and enough protein, most women notice firmer, stronger glutes within 8–12 weeks. A clear change in size and shape usually takes 4–6 months. The scale is a poor guide here, so take progress photos from the same angle every four weeks and measure your hips. Your hip thrust numbers going up is the best early sign you're on track.

Frequently asked questions

How many times a week should women train glutes?

Two to three sessions a week works best for most women. Aim for roughly 10–20 hard sets for the glutes across the week, with at least one rest day between your heaviest sessions.

Can I grow my glutes at home without a gym?

Yes, if you can keep making the exercises harder. Dumbbells, a heavy resistance band and a bench or sofa are enough for hip thrusts, split squats, Romanian deadlifts and step-ups. Bodyweight alone stops working once you can do more than about 30 reps.

How long does it take to see glute growth?

Most women notice a change in shape and strength within 8–12 weeks of consistent, progressive training with enough protein. Clear changes in size usually take 4–6 months.

Will hip thrusts and squats make my thighs bulky?

Not in the way most women fear. Building noticeable muscle is slow, and the overall look depends far more on body fat levels and exercise balance. Hip thrusts and Romanian deadlifts bias the glutes and hamstrings more than the front of the thighs.

Is glute training safe after having a baby?

Usually yes, once you have been cleared by your doctor, typically from around six weeks after birth, and after checking for pelvic floor symptoms or abdominal separation. Start with lighter loads and controlled breathing, and build up gradually with a coach trained in postnatal exercise.

The bottom line

Great glutes don't come from secret exercises. They come from a handful of proven movements, done hard, two to three times a week, with weights that go up over time and enough protein to recover. If you've been putting in the effort without seeing results, the problem is almost always the plan, not you.
