The Truth About Glutes Training — What Actually Works for Women

✍ By Chloe · 6 min read

After years of coaching women in Dubai on glutes development, I've seen the same mistakes repeated over and over. Women spending hours on hip thrusts and seeing minimal change. Women afraid to go heavy. Women following generic programmes that weren't designed for female physiology. This article is everything I know about what actually works.

← Back to Blog

Why Most Women Don't See Glutes Results

Mistake 1: Not Going Heavy Enough
Muscle grows in response to progressive tension. If you've been doing the same weight for the same reps for months, your glutes have no reason to grow. The glutes respond best to heavy compound movements — hip thrusts, Romanian deadlifts, squats — performed with progressive overload.

Mistake 2: Poor Mind-Muscle Connection
You can perform a perfect hip thrust technically and barely feel it in your glutes. Before your glutes session, spend 5 minutes on activation exercises — clamshells, banded walks, glute bridges — to wake the muscles up and establish that connection.

Mistake 3: Wrong Exercise Selection
Not all glutes exercises are created equal. Different movements target different parts of the glute complex — the gluteus maximus, medius, and minimus. Many women do only hip thrusts and squats, neglecting the glute medius which is crucial for shape, posture, and injury prevention.

Mistake 4: Insufficient Volume
The glutes can handle and require more volume than most other muscle groups. Research suggests 10-20 sets per week for most women to drive hypertrophy. If you're training glutes once a week for 4-5 sets, that's simply not enough.

The 3 Things That Actually Drive Glute Growth

1. Progressive Overload
Track your weights. Every week, aim to add a small amount of weight or an extra rep. This is non-negotiable. If you are not tracking your loads, you cannot guarantee progressive overload, and without progressive overload, your glutes will not grow.

2. Full Range of Motion
Glutes respond to being stretched under load. This is why Romanian deadlifts and deep squats are so effective — the glutes are under maximum tension at the bottom of the movement. Go deep, feel the stretch, and control the movement.

3. Adequate Protein and Calories
You cannot build muscle in a significant calorie deficit. Aim for at least 1.6-2g of protein per kg of bodyweight and ensure you are eating enough total calories to support muscle growth. Many women are chronically undereating while wondering why their glutes aren't growing.

The Best Exercises for Glute Growth

For Gluteus Maximus (the main mass):
Barbell Hip Thrust — the king of glute exercises, go heavy and squeeze hard at the top. Romanian Deadlift — excellent for the lower glute and hamstring tie-in. Bulgarian Split Squat — unilateral work that forces each glute to work independently. Sumo Deadlift — wider stance increases glute activation.

For Gluteus Medius (the side and upper glute):
Banded Side Steps, Cable Hip Abduction, Clamshells, and Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift. These are crucial for shape, posture, and the lifted look most women want.

Sample Weekly Glute Training Structure

For most women, training glutes 2-3 times per week is optimal:

Session 1 (Heavy): Barbell Hip Thrust 4x6-8, Romanian Deadlift 3x8-10, Bulgarian Split Squat 3x10 each leg.

Session 2 (Moderate): Cable Kickback 3x12-15, Hip Abduction Machine 3x15, Sumo Squat 3x12, Clamshells 3x20.

Session 3 (Optional — Pump): Banded Hip Thrust 4x20, Banded Side Steps 3x20, Single-Leg Glute Bridge 3x15 each.

How Long Until You See Results?

With consistent training, adequate protein, and progressive overload — most women start to feel a difference in 4-6 weeks and see visible changes in 8-12 weeks. Building significant glute mass takes 6-18 months of consistent work. There is no shortcut. But with the right programme, the results are absolutely achievable.
