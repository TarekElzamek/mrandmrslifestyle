Why You're Not Building Muscle (The Real Reason)

✍ By Tarek · 8 min read

I spent years training hard and seeing mediocre results before I understood what was actually holding me back. It wasn't my genetics. It wasn't my gym. It wasn't my supplements. It was three specific mistakes that I — and almost every man I've coached since — was making. Here they are.

← Back to Blog

The Muscle Building Fundamentals Most Men Skip

Building muscle is simple in theory and hard in practice. Your body will add muscle tissue only when it has three things — a training stimulus (progressive overload), adequate nutrition (protein and calories), and sufficient recovery (sleep and rest). Remove any one of these and muscle growth slows or stops entirely. Most men are failing on all three simultaneously.

Mistake 1: No Progressive Overload

Progressive overload means consistently increasing the demand on your muscles over time — more weight, more reps, more sets, better technique. Without it, your muscles adapt to the stress you're placing on them and stop growing.

Here's the test: Can you remember what weight you lifted for bench press 8 weeks ago? If not — if you're going to the gym and lifting 'about the same' every week — you almost certainly have no progressive overload and your muscles have no reason to grow.

The fix: Write down every set, every rep, every weight. Every session, aim to beat your previous performance in some way. This single habit will transform your results faster than any supplement or programme change.

Mistake 2: Not Eating Enough Protein

Protein is the building block of muscle. For muscle building, aim for 1.6-2.2g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day. For an 80kg man, that's 128-176g of protein every single day. Most men eating a normal diet get 60-80g — less than half of what's needed.

High protein foods to prioritise: chicken breast (31g per 100g), eggs (6g each), Greek yoghurt (10g per 100g), tuna (30g per can), beef (26g per 100g), whey protein (25g per scoop), cottage cheese (11g per 100g).

Track your protein for one week using MyFitnessPal. Most men are shocked by how far below target they actually are.

Mistake 3: Prioritising Variety Over Progression

Social media has convinced men they need constant variation — new exercises, new programmes, new 'hacks'. This is almost entirely false. What actually builds muscle is mastering a small number of compound movements and getting progressively stronger at them over months and years.

The exercises that build the most muscle for men: Squat, Deadlift, Bench Press, Overhead Press, Barbell Row, and Pull-up/Lat Pulldown. Master these six movements. Get strong at them. Everything else is secondary.

The Sleep Problem Nobody Talks About

Testosterone — the primary muscle-building hormone — is produced primarily during sleep. A single night of less than 6 hours reduces testosterone levels by up to 15%. In Dubai, where late nights are common and work schedules are demanding, sleep is often the missing variable.

Aim for 7-9 hours. This is not optional for serious muscle growth — it is a requirement.

What About Supplements?

The only supplements with strong research backing for muscle building are: Creatine monohydrate (3-5g per day), Whey protein (convenient protein source), Caffeine (improves training performance), and Vitamin D (commonly deficient, important for testosterone).

Everything else is largely marketing. Fix your training, nutrition, and sleep first — then consider supplements as a marginal tool.

How Long Does Muscle Building Actually Take?

In the first year of proper training, most men can gain 8-12kg of muscle. In year two, 4-6kg. In year three, 2-3kg. The men who get frustrated and quit after 3 months haven't given the process enough time to work.
