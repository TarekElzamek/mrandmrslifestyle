What Is Reformer Pilates? A Beginner's Guide

By Chloe Antoniazzi  ·  6 min read  ·  October 2026
← Back to Blog

Reformer Pilates is everywhere in Dubai right now, and one of the most common questions we get is simply: what is it? If you've seen the long machine with springs and straps and wondered whether it's for you, this guide explains it in plain words.

The short answer

Reformer Pilates is Pilates done on a machine called a reformer: a sliding carriage, adjustable springs, a footbar and straps. The springs can support you while you learn, or add resistance as you get stronger, so the same machine works for complete beginners and for strong, experienced clients.

The reformer machine, part by part

  • The carriage: the padded bed you lie, sit, kneel or stand on. It slides along the frame on wheels.
  • The springs: they connect the carriage to the frame. More or heavier springs make an exercise harder, or give you more support, depending on the movement.
  • The footbar: a bar at the end you push against with your feet or hands.
  • The straps: loops you hold or put your feet in to pull the carriage.
  • The headrest and shoulder blocks: they keep you in position so you don't slide off.
  • The sitting box: a padded box placed on the carriage for seated, kneeling and lying-on-your-front exercises, like in the photo above.

Reformer vs mat Pilates

Mat PilatesReformer Pilates
EquipmentA mat, sometimes small propsThe reformer machine
ResistanceYour body weightAdjustable springs
Support for beginnersLess: you hold every position yourselfMore: springs can assist the movement
VarietyGoodHundreds of exercises lying, kneeling, sitting and standing
Where you can do itAnywhere, including at homeIn a studio with reformers

Both are good. Mat Pilates is great at home; the reformer makes it easier to learn good form and to keep progressing, because the resistance changes with a simple spring adjustment.

Reformer, Lagree, hot Pilates and "sculpt" classes

  • Lagree uses a different machine (the Megaformer) and a slower, longer-under-tension style. It's usually taught in high-intensity group classes.
  • Hot Pilates is usually mat-based, in a heated room.
  • Sculpt or fusion classes mix Pilates with weights or cardio.
  • Classic reformer Pilates focuses on control, alignment and core strength, and it's the easiest of these to adapt to your level.

The benefits of reformer Pilates

  • A stronger core and better posture. Almost every exercise asks your deep abdominal and back muscles to work.
  • Strength without heavy weights. The springs work your glutes, legs, back and arms through a full range of movement.
  • Low impact. No jumping or pounding, which makes it gentle on the joints.
  • Flexibility and mobility. Many exercises lengthen the muscles while they work.
  • Better body awareness. You learn to move with control, which carries over to everyday life and other training.
  • Adaptable before and after pregnancy, with your doctor's approval and an instructor trained in pre and postnatal exercise.

Is reformer Pilates good for weight loss?

Reformer Pilates builds strength and tone, and it's a great way to stay active. Fat loss itself comes from your overall eating and activity across the week, so we pair sessions with simple nutrition guidance. What to wear

  • Fitted clothes, so nothing catches in the springs and your instructor can see your alignment.
  • Grip socks. Non-slip Pilates socks help you stay steady on the carriage and footbar.
  • Hair tied back and no long jewellery.

What happens in your first session

Your first session starts with a short chat about your goals, your routine and anything your body needs. Then your instructor shows you how the reformer works, how to get on and off safely, and how to breathe with the movement. You'll start with foundation exercises like footwork, bridging and arm work in straps, with the springs set for your level. By the end of 50 minutes most people feel their core and glutes working, and know exactly what to expect next time.

How often should you do reformer Pilates?

Two to three sessions a week is a good target if you want to see changes in strength and posture. Once a week still helps, especially alongside walking or other training. Consistency matters more than intensity.

Private or group?

Many Dubai studios teach the reformer in groups. Private sessions mean every exercise and spring setting is chosen for you, which makes the first weeks much easier. We explain the difference in why we only do private reformer Pilates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is reformer Pilates hard for beginners?

No. The springs can be set to support you, and your instructor chooses exercises for your level. Most beginners find the reformer easier to learn than mat Pilates.

Do I need to be flexible to start?

No. Flexibility improves as you practise; you start with the range of movement you have today.

How long until I see results?

With two to three sessions a week, many people feel stronger and stand taller within a few weeks. Visible changes usually take longer and depend on your nutrition and consistency.

Do I need grip socks for reformer Pilates?

Grip socks are recommended. Their non-slip soles keep your feet steady on the carriage and footbar.

Is reformer Pilates the same as Lagree?

No. Lagree uses a different machine and a slower, high-intensity style, usually in group classes. Reformer Pilates focuses on control, alignment and core strength.

Can I do reformer Pilates while pregnant?

Only with your doctor's approval and an instructor trained in pre and postnatal exercise, who will adapt the exercises for each stage.
