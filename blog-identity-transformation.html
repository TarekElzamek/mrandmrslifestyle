The One Identity Shift That Makes Transformations Permanent

By Chloe Antoniazzi  ·  5 min read  ·  Updated October 2026
← Back to Blog

After coaching 500+ clients through transformations, we've noticed a pattern. The clients who achieve permanent results share one thing in common — and it has nothing to do with their diet or training programme.

They made a fundamental shift in how they see themselves. And they made it before the results arrived.

The Problem with Goal-Based Thinking

"I want to lose 10kg." "I want to fit into those jeans." "I want abs by summer." These are outcome goals — and while they're useful starting points, they have a critical flaw: they end.

When you reach the goal — or don't — the motivation disappears. The diet ends. The gym sessions become less frequent. Within months, many people are back where they started, sometimes worse. This is the yo-yo cycle that plagues most people's fitness journeys.

The Identity Shift

The clients who never go back don't think of health as something they're trying to achieve. They think of it as something they are. They've made a quiet, fundamental decision: I am someone who takes care of my body.

It sounds simple. But the implications are enormous.

The Key Difference

Goal-based: "I'm trying to eat healthy." · Identity-based: "I'm someone who eats well." One is a temporary effort. The other is who you are. Every choice either confirms or contradicts your identity — and identity is far more powerful than motivation.

How to Make the Shift

Step 1: Decide Who You Want to Be

Not what you want to achieve — who you want to be. "I am someone who trains consistently." "I am someone who prioritises sleep and nutrition." "I am someone who shows up even when motivation is low."

Step 2: Cast Votes for That Identity

Every healthy choice is a vote for your new identity. You don't need to be perfect. You need to cast more votes for the person you're becoming than against them. Each training session, each protein-rich meal, each early night is a vote.

Step 3: Let the Results Follow

When identity leads, results follow naturally — and permanently. You don't need to white-knuckle through a diet because you're not dieting. You're just being who you are.

Why Motivation Alone Doesn't Last

Motivation feels powerful in the first few weeks. Then work gets busy, a holiday comes along, or progress slows down, and it fades. That's not a character flaw. Motivation is an emotion, and emotions come and go. If your fitness depends on feeling motivated, it will rise and fall with your mood.

Identity is more stable. When training and eating well are simply "what you do", you don't need to feel like it. You brush your teeth without needing to be motivated. The goal is to make looking after your body feel the same way.

Small Habits That Build a New Identity

You don't change who you are in one big moment. You change through small actions repeated so often that they become part of you. These are the habits we see make the biggest difference for our clients:

  • Schedule your workouts like meetings, and treat them as appointments with yourself.
  • Make the first step tiny. On days you don't feel like it, commit to just getting changed and starting your warm-up. Most days you'll finish the session.
  • Prepare your environment: gym bag packed the night before, protein in the fridge, water bottle on your desk.
  • Use "I am" language. "I'm someone who trains four days a week" is more powerful than "I'm trying to go to the gym."
  • Track your streaks, not just your weight. Ten sessions in a row is a result in itself.
Coach's Tip

Never miss twice. Missing one workout or one meal plan is normal. Missing two in a row is the start of a new, unwanted habit.

What to Do When You Slip Up

Everyone has off days, off weeks, even off months. Holidays, illness, family events, Ramadan, work deadlines: life happens. People with a goal-based mindset see a slip-up as proof they've failed, and give up. People with an identity-based mindset see it as a temporary break from who they are, and come back.

When you slip, don't punish yourself with extreme dieting or extra hours of cardio. Just go back to your normal routine at the very next opportunity: the next meal, the next planned session. The faster you return, the less the slip matters.

Your Environment Shapes Your Identity

It's much easier to become a person who trains when you're surrounded by people who train. That might mean a training partner, a coach who checks in, or simply following people who make you want to move rather than people who make you feel bad about yourself. It's one reason we believe so strongly in training with your partner.

How We Build This Into Coaching

With every client, we focus on habits and consistency before chasing big numbers. Weekly check-ins look at how many sessions you completed, how your eating went and how you slept, not just what the scale says. Over time, those small wins add up to a different identity, and the physical results follow and stay. You can see some of those results on our client transformations page.

What This Means for Your Transformation

The physical transformation is real — the fat loss, the muscle gain, the energy and confidence. But the clients who sustain those results long-term are the ones who stopped seeing health as a destination and started seeing it as an identity.

That shift is available to anyone. And it starts with a single decision — made not at the end of a transformation, but at the beginning of one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do most people regain weight after a diet?

Usually because the diet had an end date. When it ends, old habits return. Building an identity around healthy habits makes the results much easier to keep.

How long does it take to build a new habit?

It varies from person to person and habit to habit, often anywhere from a few weeks to a few months. Consistency matters far more than being perfect.

What should I do if I miss a workout?

Don't try to make up for it. Simply go back to your normal plan at the next session, and try never to miss twice in a row.
