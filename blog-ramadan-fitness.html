Your Complete Ramadan Fitness Guide — Don't Lose Your Progress

✍ By Tarek · 7 min read

Most coaches tell you to pause during Ramadan. We tell you to use it. Here's how our clients achieve some of their best results during the holy month — without sacrificing their fast, their energy, or their faith.

The Truth About Training During Ramadan

Every year, we hear the same thing from clients: "Should I just take Ramadan off?" The answer is no — and the data backs this up. When approached correctly, Ramadan can actually accelerate fat loss, improve discipline, and build a stronger relationship with food. The key is knowing how to adapt.

We've coached hundreds of clients in Dubai through Ramadan — men and women, beginners and advanced, those fasting from Fajr to Maghrib and those with longer fasts. Here is exactly what works.

When to Train During Ramadan

Timing your workout correctly is the most important decision you'll make during Ramadan. You have three windows:

Option 1: Before Suhoor (recommended for fat loss)
Training in a fasted state — before your pre-dawn meal — maximises fat burning. Your body has been in a fasted state for hours and will use fat as its primary fuel source. Keep the session to 30-45 minutes of moderate intensity. Don't push to failure as your recovery will be limited.

Option 2: After Iftar (recommended for muscle retention)
Training 60-90 minutes after breaking your fast gives you fuel to train hard. This is the best option if your goal is building or maintaining muscle. You'll have carbohydrates and protein available and can push intensity without compromising the fast.

Option 3: Just Before Iftar
Training in the final hour before Iftar works for some people but not others. You're at your most depleted so keep sessions short — 20-30 minutes maximum — and focus on low-to-moderate intensity movement like walking or light resistance training.

Nutrition Strategy: Suhoor and Iftar

Suhoor — The Foundation Meal
This is your most important meal. It needs to sustain you for 12-16 hours. Prioritise slow-digesting carbohydrates (oats, brown rice, sweet potato), high protein (eggs, Greek yoghurt, chicken — aim for at least 40g), healthy fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil), fibre (vegetables, fruit, legumes), and maximum hydration — drink 750ml-1 litre of water at Suhoor. This is non-negotiable in Dubai's heat.

Iftar — Break the Fast Correctly
Break your fast with 2-3 dates and water. Wait 15-20 minutes before your main meal. Build your Iftar plate: 40% protein, 30% complex carbohydrates, 30% vegetables. Avoid deep-fried foods, excessive sugar, and heavy creamy dishes — these spike insulin, cause energy crashes, and store fat.

Hydration: The Dubai Challenge

Dehydration is the number one performance killer during Ramadan in Dubai, where temperatures exceed 35°C. Between Iftar and Suhoor you need to consume at least 2.5-3 litres of water. Space it out — don't drink it all at once. Add electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium) if you're training, as you'll lose more through sweat.

Avoid excessive caffeine. One coffee after Iftar is fine but more than that will increase water loss and disrupt sleep — which is already shortened during Ramadan.

Adjusting Your Training Volume

Reduce your training volume by 20-30% during Ramadan. This is not weakness — it is intelligent programming. Your recovery capacity is reduced due to disrupted sleep and calorie restriction. Maintaining intensity while reducing volume is the smart approach.

Think of it this way: you are maintaining, not building. Ramadan is not the time to set personal bests. It is the time to preserve what you've built and come out of the month healthy and ready to push hard again.

What Our Clients Actually Achieve During Ramadan

When our clients follow this approach, here is what we consistently see: fat loss of 2-4kg during Ramadan (particularly visceral fat), maintained or slightly reduced muscle mass, improved relationship with hunger and food, and better discipline that carries over after Eid.

The clients who struggle are those who abandon their programme entirely and use Ramadan as an excuse to overeat at Iftar. Don't be that person.

After Ramadan: The Eid Trap

More transformations are destroyed in the two weeks after Ramadan than during the entire month. The Eid celebrations, the gatherings, the return to normal eating — it can undo 4 weeks of great work very quickly. Our recommendation: allow yourself 2-3 days of celebration, then return to your programme immediately. Don't let Eid become a month-long excuse.
