Chapter One

The Dubai Nutrition Code

Living in Dubai is incredible — but it comes with unique nutritional challenges that most fitness guides completely ignore. Endless brunches. Late-night shawarma. Ramadan. Business dinners. All-inclusive resorts. If you've ever felt like your environment was working against your goals, this chapter is for you.

The Dubai Eating Environment

Portion sizes in Dubai restaurants are enormous. Social eating is central to the culture. And the best-tasting food often comes with the highest calorie counts. The solution isn't to avoid it all — it's to eat smart within it.

The Restaurant Strategy (Works Everywhere)

  • Order protein first: grilled chicken, fish, meat, eggs — make this the anchor of your meal
  • Ask for sauces on the side — most restaurant calories hide in dressings and sauces
  • Eat until 80% full, not 100% — it takes 20 minutes for fullness to register
  • At brunches: protein and salad first, treat dishes last — limits total intake naturally
  • Drink water before ordering — often hunger is thirst in disguise

Ramadan: Your Physique Is Not Doomed

Most people either restrict too hard during Ramadan and lose muscle, or eat too freely during iftar and gain fat. Here's the approach that works:

Iftar
Break fast with dates + water, then protein-heavy meal. Avoid overdoing carbs immediately — your digestion needs warming up.
Suhoor
This is your most important meal. Slow-digesting carbs (oats, whole grains) + protein + healthy fats = sustained energy through fasting hours.

Coach Tarek's Ramadan Rule

"I tell every client: Ramadan is not a reason to pause your transformation. Adjust your training to evenings after iftar, hit your protein targets in two meals instead of six, and stay hydrated between iftar and suhoor. Some of our most impressive results happen during Ramadan."

Travel & Business Dinners

Dubai is a hub city — many clients travel constantly. The rule here is simple: keep protein high, keep alcohol minimal, stay active wherever you are. Hotels have gyms. Walking meetings are underrated. You can do this.

Chapter Two

Macro Mastery Made Simple

You don't need to count every calorie forever. But understanding macros — protein, carbohydrates, and fat — is the foundation of any body transformation. This chapter gives you your starting numbers.

Why Macros Matter More Than "Clean Eating"

The fitness world loves to label foods as "clean" or "dirty." But your body runs on energy and nutrients — not labels. 200 calories of chicken and 200 calories of chocolate affect your weight almost identically. What matters is totals, not individual choices.

Your Starting Macro Targets

  • Protein: 1.8–2.2g per kg of bodyweight — this is your #1 priority
  • Fat loss: Create a 300–500 calorie deficit from your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)
  • Muscle gain: Eat at TDEE or 200–300 calories above it
  • Carbs: Fill remaining calories after protein and fat are set
  • Fat: Minimum 0.7g per kg bodyweight — important for hormones

Calculate Your TDEE (Your Calorie Baseline)

TDEE = Basal Metabolic Rate × Activity Multiplier. Your BMR is roughly your bodyweight in kg × 22 (for women) or × 24 (for men). Then multiply by your activity level:

×1.2
Sedentary (desk job, little exercise)
×1.55
Moderately active (3–5 workouts/week)
×1.375
Lightly active (1–3 workouts/week)
×1.725
Very active (hard training 6–7 days)

Example: 75kg Woman, Moderately Active

BMR = 75 × 22 = 1,650 kcal
TDEE = 1,650 × 1.55 = 2,557 kcal
Fat loss target = 2,557 − 400 = ~2,150 kcal
Protein = 75 × 2.0 = 150g/day

Simple. Start here. Adjust based on results after 2–3 weeks.

The One Metric That Changes Everything

Daily protein intake. If you hit your protein target every day, everything else becomes easier. Protein keeps you full, preserves muscle during fat loss, and drives muscle growth. Make it your non-negotiable.

Chapter Four

Training That Actually Works

Most people either do too much too soon and burn out, or too little and wonder why nothing changes. This template has been tested on hundreds of our clients. It works — whether you're at a gym or at home.

The Beginner–Intermediate Template (3 Days/Week)

For your first 4–8 weeks, 3 sessions per week is optimal. More is not better when you're building the habit. Each session takes 45–60 minutes.

Day A — Upper Body Push + Pull

  • Push-ups or Chest Press — 3×10–12
  • Dumbbell Row or Cable Row — 3×10–12
  • Shoulder Press — 3×10–12
  • Lat Pulldown or Assisted Pull-up — 3×8–10
  • Tricep Dips or Pushdown — 2×12–15
  • Bicep Curl — 2×12–15

Day B — Lower Body + Core

  • Squat (Goblet, Barbell, or Bodyweight) — 4×8–12
  • Romanian Deadlift — 3×10–12
  • Glute Bridge or Hip Thrust — 3×12–15
  • Walking Lunges — 3×10/leg
  • Plank — 3×30–45 seconds
  • Dead Bug — 3×8/side

Day C — Full Body + Cardio Finish

  • Deadlift or Trap Bar — 3×6–8
  • Incline Dumbbell Press — 3×10–12
  • Bulgarian Split Squat — 3×10/leg
  • Cable or Band Pull-Apart — 3×15
  • 20 min steady-state cardio (walk, bike, rower) — keep HR 130–150

Progressive Overload: The Only Rule That Matters

Every week, try to do slightly more than last week. One extra rep. 2.5kg more on the bar. One fewer rest second. This is called progressive overload — and it is the entire scientific basis of how your body changes. Without it, you plateau. With it, you grow indefinitely.

The Couples Challenge

Research shows people who train with a partner are 65% more likely to stick to their programme. Now imagine that partner is your significant other — someone who is equally invested in your success and present every single day.

The 30-Day Couples Challenge

This challenge is designed for couples to do together. You'll train on the same schedule, support each other's nutrition, and compete in a friendly way that makes the process genuinely enjoyable.

  • ✓
    Share your weekly training schedule with each other — hold each other to it
  • ✓
    Meal prep together on Sundays — double efficiency, double accountability
  • ✓
    Track each other's weekly check-ins — weight, photos, performance metrics
  • ✓
    Create a couples goal (e.g. "We'll go on a beach holiday in 12 weeks when we hit X")
  • ✓
    Celebrate non-scale victories together — new personal bests, better sleep, more energy
  • ✓
    Never criticise each other's progress — only encourage

Couples Training Tips from Tarek & Chloe

  • Train at the same time, but don't always do identical workouts — your goals may differ
  • Cook together whenever possible — nutritional goals are 10x easier when you align
  • Use a shared WhatsApp group to log meals and workouts — it creates social accountability
  • One person should never make it harder for the other to succeed (no bringing junk food home)

Chapter Six

The Mindset Shift

Every client who has ever achieved a remarkable transformation with us had one thing in common. It wasn't their genetics, their schedule, or their starting point. It was a single belief shift that changed how they showed up every single day.

The Shift: From "I'm Trying to Change" to "This Is Who I Am"

Most people approach fitness as something external — a challenge to overcome, a phase to get through, a goal to tick off. And that's exactly why most people quit. Because when it gets hard, it's easy to say "this isn't really me anyway."

The clients who succeed permanently make a different move. They decide — before the results come — that they are a healthy, active person. They act from identity, not motivation.

Identity-Based Action

  • "I don't skip workouts" instead of "I'm trying to be more consistent"
  • "I'm someone who hits their protein" instead of "I'm trying to eat better"
  • "I'm a person who prioritises their health" instead of "I'm on a diet right now"
  • Vote for your new identity with every small action — consistency compounds

Dealing With Hard Days

Hard days will come. You'll miss a session. You'll eat the wrong thing. You'll feel unmotivated. The question is never "will I have a bad day" — it's "what do I do on the bad day?"

  • ✓
    Never miss twice — one missed session is a blip, two is the start of a habit
  • ✓
    Lower the bar on hard days — a 15-minute walk counts. Done beats perfect.
  • ✓
    Progress is not linear — accept the plateaus as part of the process
  • ✓
    Talk to your coach — we've seen it all, and we're here for these moments exactly

The Truth About Motivation

"Motivation is a feeling. Feelings come and go. Discipline is a skill. Skills are built with repetition. Stop waiting to feel motivated and start building the skill of showing up regardless. The motivation will follow the action — not the other way around."

— Chloe
